Research has two halves that most lists mix together. One half is the AI layer: assistants
that read, search, summarise, and synthesise. The other is the scholarly infrastructure that
layer sits on, which is older, mostly free, and does not market itself. This list covers both,
and says which is which.

The distinction matters because the failure mode of AI research tools is confident invention.
Entries here are grouped by what they are actually connected to: live web results, an indexed
corpus of papers, your own uploaded documents, or nothing at all. No entry claims citation
support unless the product documents it.

Maintained by [TiorAI](https://tiorai.com/), which catalogues AI tools for a living.

## AI search and answer engines

Search that returns a written answer with the sources it drew on.

- **[Andi](https://andisearch.com/)** — Ad-free search that answers in conversation and shows the pages it read. `Free` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Genspark](https://www.genspark.ai/)** — Runs a search agent per question and assembles the findings into a single page. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Kagi](https://kagi.com/)** — Subscription search with no advertising, plus an assistant that cites its results. `Paid` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Komo](https://komo.ai/)** — Lightweight answer engine aimed at quick topic scans rather than long reports. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Liner](https://liner.com/)** — Search assistant that keeps highlights and cited passages in a personal library. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `Browser Extension`
- **[Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/)** — Answers questions from live web results with inline citations you can open and check. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API` · [vs You.com](https://tiorai.com/compare/perplexity-vs-you-com/)
- **[Phind](https://www.phind.com/)** — Answer engine tuned for technical questions, quoting documentation and code sources. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[You.com](https://you.com/)** — Search and assistant combined, with modes that trade speed against source depth. `Freemium` `Web` `API`

## Deep research agents

Multi-step agents that browse for a while and return a sourced report.

- **[ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/)** — Its deep research mode browses for several minutes and returns a cited written report. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Claude](https://claude.com/product/overview)** — Research mode searches, reads, and reports back with links to what it used. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/)** — Deep Research plans a browsing route, works through it, and returns a sourced brief. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[GPT Researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher)** — Open-source agent that runs a research loop and outputs a referenced report. `Open Source` `Web` `API` `CLI`
- **[Grok](https://grok.com/)** — DeepSearch mode works through web and social sources before writing its answer. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`
- **[Kimi](https://www.kimi.com/)** — Long-context assistant that reads many documents or pages in a single research pass. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Manus](https://manus.im/)** — General agent that carries a research task through to a finished deliverable. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[STORM](https://storm.genie.stanford.edu/)** — Stanford research prototype that drafts a Wikipedia-style article from cited sources. `Free` `Web`

## Academic search engines

The scholarly indexes underneath most of the AI research tools listed here.

- **[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/)** — Open preprint server where most machine learning work appears before peer review. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[CORE](https://core.ac.uk/)** — Aggregates open access papers from repositories worldwide and exposes the full text. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/)** — Life sciences literature search covering abstracts, full text, preprints, and grants. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/)** — The broadest scholarly index, with citation counts and links to available copies. `Free` `Web`
- **[Lens.org](https://www.lens.org/)** — Links scholarly literature to patent records, which few other indexes attempt. `Freemium` `Web` `API`
- **[OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/)** — Open catalogue of works, authors, and institutions, free to query at full scale. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)** — The reference index for biomedical literature, run by the US National Library of Medicine. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/)** — Scholarly search with extracted figures, citation context, and a well documented API. `Free` `Web` `API`

## Paper discovery and citation mapping

Finding the next paper by following how the literature connects.

- **[Connected Papers](https://www.connectedpapers.com/)** — Builds a similarity graph around one paper so neighbouring work becomes visible. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/)** — Tracks new computer science preprints and explains what each one claims. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Iris.ai](https://iris.ai/)** — Maps a research question to related literature and filters it down by concept. `Freemium` `Web` `API`
- **[Litmaps](https://www.litmaps.com/)** — Citation maps that update as new work cites the papers you already track. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Open Knowledge Maps](https://openknowledgemaps.org/)** — Charity-run service that clusters search results into a visual overview of a field. `Free` `Web`
- **[Paper Digest](https://www.paperdigest.org/)** — Conference and journal digests that condense each paper to its central claim. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[ResearchRabbit](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/)** — Follows a seed set of papers and keeps recommending related work as you refine it. `Free` `Web`
- **[Scinapse](https://www.scinapse.io/)** — Academic search with author, journal, and institution filters over a large index. `Freemium` `Web`

## Reading and summarising papers

Getting through a PDF faster without losing what it actually said.

- **[Anara](https://anara.com/)** — Workspace for reading sources and asking questions that answer from the text itself. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[AskYourPDF](https://askyourpdf.com/)** — Uploads a document and answers questions against it with page references. `Freemium` `Web` `API`
- **[ChatDOC](https://chatdoc.com/)** — Document reader that anchors every answer to the passage it came from. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Explainpaper](https://www.explainpaper.com/)** — Highlight a confusing passage in a paper and get it explained in plainer terms. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google/)** — Grounds every answer in the sources you upload, and refuses to go beyond them. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Humata](https://www.humata.ai/)** — Question answering across a folder of documents rather than one file at a time. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Scholarcy](https://www.scholarcy.com/)** — Turns a paper into a summary card with figures, references, and key claims. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[SciSpace](https://scispace.com/)** — Reads papers alongside you and explains equations, tables, and methods on request. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`

## Literature review and evidence synthesis

Screening, extracting, and synthesising across many papers at once.

- **[ASReview](https://asreview.nl/)** — Open-source active learning tool that reorders screening so relevant records surface early. `Open Source` `Windows` `macOS` `Linux` `CLI`
- **[Consensus](https://consensus.app/)** — Searches papers and reports how much of the literature supports a given claim. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Covidence](https://www.covidence.org/)** — Systematic review platform for screening, full-text review, and extraction with a team. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[DistillerSR](https://www.distillersr.com/)** — Regulated-grade review software with the audit trails that submissions require. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Elicit](https://elicit.com/)** — Extracts study characteristics into a table across dozens of papers at once. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Rayyan](https://www.rayyan.ai/)** — Collaborative screening tool widely used for systematic reviews in health research. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Scite](https://scite.ai/)** — Shows whether later papers supported, mentioned, or disputed the work being cited. `Freemium` `Web` `API` `Browser Extension`
- **[Undermind](https://www.undermind.ai/)** — Runs a long structured search pass and reports how thoroughly it covered the topic. `Freemium` `Web`

## Fact checking and verification

Checking a claim, an image, or a citation before you repeat it.

- **[ClaimBuster](https://idir.claimbuster.org/)** — University research tool that scores sentences for how check-worthy they are. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[Factiverse](https://www.factiverse.ai/)** — Detects checkable claims in a text and retrieves supporting or contradicting sources. `Freemium` `Web` `API`
- **[Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/)** — UK charity publishing checked claims, with the working shown for each one. `Free` `Web`
- **[Google Fact Check Explorer](https://toolbox.google.com/factcheck/explorer)** — Searches published fact checks from accredited organisations in one place. `Free` `Web` `API`
- **[InVID WeVerify](https://weverify.eu/)** — Verification toolkit for video and images, including keyframe and reverse-search checks. `Free` `Browser Extension`
- **[Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/)** — Reports retractions and maintains the database behind most retraction alerts. `Free` `Web`
- **[Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/)** — Long-running claim archive, useful for tracing where a story started. `Free` `Web`
- **[TinEye](https://tineye.com/)** — Reverse image search that finds the earliest indexed copy of a picture. `Freemium` `Web` `API` `Browser Extension`

## How tools are selected

A tool qualifies when it materially helps with one of: finding sources, searching the
literature, reading and understanding a document, synthesising evidence across many sources,
organising what you found, analysing data, or checking a claim.

### The rule that does most of the work here

**A general chatbot is not a research tool.** Answering a question from memory is not
research, and a list that treats it as research is where bad citations come from.

A general assistant appears here only in [Deep research agents](#deep-research-agents), and
only because it ships a distinct multi-step research mode that browses, works for several
minutes, and returns a report with links. That is a specific, checkable capability. It is not
the same as the chat box on the same site, and this repository does not treat it as the same.

### What the categories are telling you

The grouping is the substance of this list. Each category answers a different question about
where an answer came from:

- **AI search and answer engines** are connected to the live web and cite pages.
- **Deep research agents** browse for longer and return something report-shaped.
- **Academic search engines** query an indexed corpus of scholarly works.
- **Reading and summarising papers** answers only from documents you supplied.
- **Literature review and evidence synthesis** works across a screened set of studies.
- **Fact checking and verification** checks a specific claim, image, or citation.

A tool that answers from live search is doing something different from a tool that answers
from an indexed corpus, which is different again from one that answers only from your own
uploads. Knowing which you are using is most of the skill.

### What is excluded

Writing assistants with no source layer. Products whose research claim is a homepage
adjective. Paper mills, essay-writing services, and anything sold on the promise of avoiding
reading. AI-content detectors, which are widely marketed to academics and are not reliable
enough to be listed as verification tools. Note-taking apps with no research workflow, which
belong in `awesome-ai-productivity-tools`.

Entries are ordered alphabetically inside each category. Position carries no meaning, and
there is no paid placement, sponsorship, or affiliate link anywhere in this repository.

## Disclaimer

Everything listed is built and operated by a third party, not by TiorAI. Pricing, plan
limits, corpus coverage, and API terms change without notice.

Nothing here is a claim about accuracy. Every tool in this list can produce a wrong summary,
a mis-stated finding, or a citation that does not support the sentence attached to it. That
includes the tools whose entire pitch is that they do not. Open the source before you cite
it, and check that it says what the tool claims it says.

Several entries are subject to institutional access, licensing, and publisher terms. Check
what your institution permits before uploading a paywalled paper to a third-party service.

Listing here is editorial selection, not endorsement.
